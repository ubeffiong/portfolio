Health-in-a-Box (HnB): Hospital Management Information System

CTO / Product Manager · Health-in-a-Box Solutions
Deployed

A modular hospital information system that digitises clinical, diagnostic, administrative and financial workflows in about 25 hospitals. It is my largest hospital-level digital health product.

  • ~25 hospitals
  • 4,500+ health workers trained
  • 25 federal institutions on the FMoH dashboard
  • On-prem · cloud · hybrid deployment
Read the full case studyClose case study
The problem

Disconnected systems, manual registers, duplicate patient records, fragmented laboratory and radiology workflows and poor revenue visibility, often in facilities with unreliable connectivity. These delay care and let revenue leak.

My role
  • Turned hospital operational requirements into product features and implementation plans; prioritised modules and coordinated engineering.
  • Designed and reviewed software architecture and database structures.
  • Coordinated hospital onboarding and deployment; integrated laboratory analysers, imaging and payment services.
  • Led training, adoption and troubleshooting; wrote deployment documentation for on-premises, cloud and hybrid environments.
  • Aligned the product with data-protection, security and interoperability requirements.
What was built
  • EMR: registration, encounters, history, diagnoses, prescriptions and longitudinal records.
  • LIS: orders, specimens, results and analyser interfaces that cut manual transcription.
  • RIS & PACS: radiology workflows with DICOM imaging linked to the record.
  • Pharmacy: prescribing, dispensing, inventory and drug-interaction alerts.
  • Specialist services: ART clinics, IVF, dialysis, cardiology, theatre, ECG, echocardiography and Holter monitoring.
  • Billing & revenue: bills, payments and balances, the foundation for CareWallet and SmartCare Card.
Products built on HnB
  • Informatics Dashboard (Product Manager): deployed at the Federal Ministry of Health. It aggregates visits, new patients, payer mix, wait times, doctor-to-patient ratio and treatment cost from 25 federal health institutions (15 FTHs, 7 FMCs, 3 SHs). See screenshot
  • FastCare: telemedicine app linked to HMIS records and appointments.
  • HIMIS: insurance workflows connecting providers, HMOs and state and public schemes.
Architecture & technology

Configurable workflows, role-based permissions and secure record access across hospitals with different needs. Offline synchronisation with conflict resolution, retries, audit and duplicate-transaction controls. Interoperability direction: HL7, FHIR and terminology mapping.

SORMAS Optimisation & Re-engineering: national disease surveillance

Health Informatics Expert / Technical Lead · IHVN with NCDC · U.S. CDC-funded SECURE-Nigeria
Deployed

Modernisation of Nigeria's electronic surveillance and outbreak-management platform: a version upgrade, re-engineered disease forms, performance work and an NCDC team trained to run it. National go-live was on 27 February 2026, across all 774 LGAs.

  • v1.82 → v1.101 upgrade
  • 18 disease-specific forms
  • ~25% faster form loading
  • 15+ min → <3 min large downloads
Read the full case studyClose case study
The problem

A national system covering many diseases, large record volumes and users at federal, state and LGA level. It had fallen 19 versions behind, its forms loaded slowly, disease workflows were inconsistent, and maintenance depended on outside developers.

My role

Technical lead for software optimisation, form re-engineering, integration, problem-solving and workforce capacity strengthening (implementation March–September 2025).

What was done
  • Version upgrade from 1.82.0 to 1.101.0, covering compatibility, data integrity, regression testing and deployment procedures.
  • Form re-engineering: replaced ReactJS case investigation forms with JavaServer Faces (JSF) implementations for performance and fit.
  • 18 disease-specific forms with validation rules matching national paper forms.
  • Laboratory results linked to cases by EPID number, a faster download manager and a contingency line-list upload.
  • Multi-level user validation (a 23-item instrument) to prioritise 40+ issues before go-live.
Capacity & governance

Trained 14 of 15 targeted NCDC Public Health Informatics personnel to maintain, develop and troubleshoot the platform. Co-designed the governance framework NCDC adopted: Git version control, change requests, and dev → staging → production.

  • Java

  • PostgreSQL

DAVT: Data Analytics and Visualization Tool (SECURE-Nigeria)

Architect / Technical Lead · IHVN & NCDC
Deployed

NCDC's surveillance analytics layer, which turns SORMAS and other data into dashboards, the Weekly Epidemiological Report, alerts and decisions. It has been used in all 36 states and the FCT since January 2024.

  • 6 → 37 states + FCT

Read the full case studyClose case study
The problem

Surveillance data was collected but hard to turn into evidence. Reports were compiled by hand and late, there was no single outbreak view, decisions were recorded manually, and there was no automatic cross-check of SORMAS against other sources.

Objectives
  • Integrate surveillance sources and reliably extract and transform records.
  • Provide interactive dashboards and trend analysis by place and time.
  • Make routine reporting efficient and consistent.
  • Strengthen traceability with audit trails and controlled access.

Extraction schedules, pagination, schema compatibility, failed requests, duplicate prevention, separating missing values from zero counts, and traceability from results back to source records.

CareWallet: healthcare fintech, payment orchestration & revenue

Product & Systems Design Lead
In development

A payment layer connecting patients, hospitals, EMRs and payment providers. It links every transaction to the patient, facility and service, and keeps the hospital in control of its own finances.

  • 3 wallet layers
  • Any EMR, not only HnB
  • Multi-processor strategy
Read the full case studyClose case study
The problem

Deposits, charges, cash, transfers, outstanding bills, refunds and settlement often sit in disconnected processes. The result is delayed revenue recognition, mismatched balances and transactions that are hard to trace.

Wallet ecosystem
  • Patient e-wallet: deposits, payments, refunds and balance.
  • Hospital settlement wallet: maps collected funds to the facility.
  • TSA or designated account: onward settlement under each hospital's approved arrangement.
Ledger design

The HnB/EMR keeps the patient-level ledger as the source of truth. It records funding, charges, allocations, reversals, refunds and settlement advice, using idempotency keys, an immutable history and explicit reversals. An API call is never treated as proof that a payment happened.

Integrations
  • Moniepoint: reserved-account (pool and settlement with daily advice) and wallet models; webhooks, statements and settlement APIs.
  • Parkway: virtual and static wallets, POS SDK.
  • Wema Bank / ALATPay: co-branded Verve cards and payment integration.
Beyond HnB

Deployable at facilities on other EMRs through APIs for patient identification, billing references, payment initiation, status, refunds and reconciliation.

  • Payment orchestration
  • Ledger design
  • Webhooks
  • Reconciliation

PlasBench, PlasmiCord & PlasmidTrace: plasmid-AMR bioinformatics

Bioinformatics Analyst & Developer · Creator
Prototype

A connected research portfolio on how antimicrobial-resistance plasmids spread. It covers benchmarking plasmid reconstruction, mapping plasmid relatedness, and attaching honest confidence to transmission signals.

  • 3 linked tools
  • 4 reconstruction tools benchmarked
  • Reproducible and provenance-tracked
Read the full case studyClose case study
Why it matters

Plasmids can carry resistance genes between bacteria. Finding the same gene in two isolates does not prove transmission. That needs plasmid similarity, genomic context, bacterial relatedness, metadata and careful interpretation.

The three tools
  • PlasBench: benchmarks MOB-recon, Platon, plasmidSPAdes and gplas against truth sets (precision, recall, F1, failure modes). GitHub
  • PlasmiCord: pairwise plasmid distances, clustering and isolate-sharing networks to find candidate horizontal spread. GitHub
  • PlasmidTrace: links reconstruction reliability with sharing evidence to produce confidence scores; needs external validation.
Shared pipeline
  1. Sequencing data & metadata (FASTQ, FASTA, assemblies)
  2. QC & genome processing
  3. Annotation & resistance profiling (Prokka, ResFinder, CARD)
  4. Plasmid comparison, clustering & phylogeny
  5. Evidence-based interpretation & reproducible reports
Research quality

Records software and database versions, parameters, input checksums, QC metrics and output provenance. Keeps observed data separate from inferred relationships, documents uncertainty, and never treats a database match as proof of resistance or transmission.

  • Python
  • Ubuntu / Bash
  • Prokka
  • ResFinder
  • CARD
  • Phylogenetics
  • Genomic epidemiology

CODA: Clinical Ontology, Discovery and Assistance

Creator · Product & Technical Lead
In development

A clinical terminology intelligence platform that searches, validates and explains clinical codes across terminologies. Its core is deterministic and versioned; AI assists, but is never the source of truth for a code.

  • ICD-11 · LOINC · RxNorm · SNOMED CT and UMLS sources
  • 6 public SDKs
  • .NET 10 + React workbench
  • Audited coding decisions
Read the full case studyClose case study
The problem

Clinical data is only as good as its codes. Teams code by hand across ICD, LOINC, RxNorm and SNOMED CT with no shared validation, no audit of why a code was chosen, and no safe way to manage local codes or mappings.

What CODA does
  • Search, look up, validate and explain codes across terminologies, served live from their publishers (WHO ICD-11, LOINC, RxNorm and other UMLS sources) under each organisation's licences.
  • Every selection and review is recorded as an audited decision.
  • Local dictionaries and mappings with four-eyes review, plus value sets.
  • An AI phrase assistant that suggests candidates; deterministic rules decide.
  • An EMR sandbox and embeddable components to drop terminology search into any clinical app.
Architecture & engineering
  • API: .NET 10 modular monolith, PostgreSQL, OIDC/JWT (authorisation code + PKCE), role-based access and a full audit trail.
  • Web: React + TypeScript coding workbench and reusable components.
  • Resilience: a local copy of every terminology, refreshed monthly, answers when a live source is down.
  • Interoperability: FHIR adapter; SNOMED CT through an evaluated terminology engine.
  • Quality: CI with build, tests, vulnerability scans, Playwright end-to-end journeys, load tests and observability.
SDKs for integrators

Thin, dependency-light clients with one shared contract in C#/.NET, TypeScript/JavaScript, Python, Java, Go and Rust, released with verified checksums.

  • .NET 10
  • PostgreSQL
  • React
  • TypeScript
  • OIDC

Creator · Personal project
Prototype

An enterprise patient body-charting engine. Clinicians document findings directly on an interactive 3D/2D anatomical model instead of paper diagrams, across nursing, emergency, surgery, dermatology, wound care, burns, pain and obstetrics.

  • 3D + 2D print-safe charting
  • 8 body habitus variants
  • SNOMED-coded body regions

Clinical capabilities
  • Smart markers, freehand drawing, region shading and severity heatmaps.
  • Burn surface area (adult rule of nines, paediatric tables, Lund-Browder) with Parkland fluid calculation.
  • Wound tracking with healing trends; pain mapping with structured OPQRST capture.
