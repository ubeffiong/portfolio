SORMAS optimisation, implementation & validation · 2025 – 2026
  • Benchmarked the national installation (v1.82.0) against v1.101.0, identifying 19 missed release cycles, technical debt, workflow misalignment and governance gaps across 44 disease modules.
  • Upgraded to v1.101.0 with all historical data migrated without loss, and merged the FCT's separate SORMAS instance into the national system.
  • Led the redesign of 18 disease-specific electronic Case Investigation Forms that match NCDC's approved paper forms field for field, replacing one general form. They cover AFP, cholera, COVID-19, CSM, dengue, diphtheria, Ebola, influenza, Lassa fever, measles, Mpox, neonatal tetanus, noma, PRDS, rubella, yellow fever, chikungunya and other viral haemorrhagic fevers.
  • Linked laboratory results to cases automatically by EPID number; automated outbreak-threshold alerts and national case-classification rules.
  • Restored connections to other systems, added a record-level anonymised case feed to DAVT (replacing a summary-only link), and prepared SORMAS for exchange with laboratory information systems.
  • Specified epidemiological and clinical variables, mandatory fields, conditional logic and validation rules aligned with Nigeria's IDSR guidelines.
  • Configured data-quality controls: date sequencing (onset before report), age-based criteria (e.g. AFP under 15), laboratory-result linkage and duplicate-case prevention.
  • Diagnosed and remediated Download Manager failures (timeouts, empty exports, crashes) with a new download manager that filters by disease, area, dates and fields; large downloads fell from over 15 minutes to under 3.
  • Built a contingency line-list upload (standardised CSV/Excel) for continuity during downtime or API instability.
  • Designed a phased mixed-methods evaluation: a desk review, implementation-stage checks and multi-user testing with a 23-item instrument and qualitative guides covering 25 participants across four tiers.
  • Analysed results in Stata 15 (descriptive statistics, 95% CIs, binary aggregation) and thematically; surfaced residual gaps in alert automation and conflicting-data detection for post-go-live work.
  • Supported a validation workshop (Jul 2025) and structured user acceptance testing (Jan–Feb 2026); the full issue log and codebase were handed over into NCDC's own repository.
DAVT: national surveillance analytics & visualisation
  • Led end-to-end development of a secure web platform for NCDC and state PHEOCs. It launched in September 2022 in six IHVN-supported states (Bauchi, Edo, Enugu, Kaduna, Lagos, Ondo) and expanded with NCDC to all 36 states and the FCT in January 2024.
  • Ran participatory co-design through six months of inception workshops with NCDC leadership and TWGs, WHO Nigeria, State MoHs, U.S. CDC and frontline officers.
  • Connected five data sources: SORMAS (live API), IDSR-002 weekly facility summaries, state line lists, DHIS2 at NPHCDA and NAFDAC's MedSafety app, the last two for vaccine-safety (AEFI) triangulation.
  • Platform of 10 modules: summary report, aggregate reports, case-based analytics, automated Weekly Epidemiological Report, data-quality scoring, line-list upload, concurrent trends, data triangulation, a live EOC wallboard and a decision tracker.
  • Kept surveillance running during outages by letting every analysis switch between SORMAS and uploaded line-list data.
  • Prepared the sustainability strategy, the migration plan to NCDC infrastructure and technical training for NCDC developers.
  • Built threshold monitoring for priority diseases (Lassa fever, Yellow Fever, Measles and others), with an auditable alert → response → resolution trail for after-action reviews.
  • Produced analytical briefs and technical reports for NCDC and U.S. CDC on system performance, response timeliness and IHR progress.
New DAVT modules (designed & built; awaiting NCDC adoption)
  • Digital Weekly Epidemiological Report: a complete draft (tables, maps, charts) is ready the Monday after each week closes, then moves through a five-step approval workflow to a locked, versioned publication.
  • Automatic disease situation reports: generated for any disease directly from SORMAS case records, inside an approval workflow owned by each disease working group.
  • National IDSR Compliance Index (NICI): a monthly 0–100 surveillance-performance score for the country, each state and each LGA, built from seven weighted indicators and including the 7-1-7 outbreak timeliness target.
  • Advanced analytics: statistical tests, seven transmission models, four reproduction-number estimators, short-term forecasts, a "what if" simulator and a shareable research workspace, all on live data with no external licences.
Data quality, epidemiological analysis & strategic information
  • Implemented SDQM alongside DAVT: a pilot in Ondo, Sokoto, Ebonyi and the FCT (Oct 2023 – Sep 2024) tracking four quality measures across 10 priority diseases, with monthly reviews using DAVT's concurrent-trends view. Later expanded to seven states.
  • Analysed Yellow Fever concordance (2022–2025) and Lassa fever trends, CFR and cross-system concordance: 30,511 paper vs 31,959 SORMAS cases across six SDQM states.
  • Support IHVN Strategic Information on the Nigeria Medical Record System (NMRS) for HIV/TB programming: workflows, data quality, reporting and data use.
Workforce development & governance · May 2025 – Sep 2025
  • Designed a Learn–Do–Sustain programme for NCDC PHI officers: 12 weekly virtual sessions plus two five-day in-person bootcamps. All 8 participants in the first workshop completed a Java project, and all 15 in the second built a working database application.
  • Curriculum: HTML/CSS, JavaScript, Java OOP, Java MVC, PostgreSQL database design and SORMAS codebase integration.
  • Co-designed a governance framework adopted by NCDC: Git/GitHub version control, documented change requests, dev → staging → production environments and monthly performance reporting.
  • Handed over a documentation repository (upgrade and troubleshooting SOPs, manuals, module guides, a post-training technical guide) and a milestone-based transition roadmap.
